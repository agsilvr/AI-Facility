# Owner-supplied design guidance — 30 October 2026

Saved from the owner’s document. Internal creative guidance; provider specifications still control production. Prior Reading Cat artwork is superseded and production remains paused.

Etsy design guidelines and mistake checks

Purpose

This manual gives the designer and reviewer in our own Etsy automation system a shared quality standard. It covers the artwork, physical product placement, print file and listing mockups. A good illustration is only one part of an acceptable product.

The initial direction is cozy reading gifts, including cat-and-book designs, on sweatshirts and related print-on-demand products. Apply general quality checks to every product. Apply collection-specific style choices only where the product brief calls for them.

These are proposed internal design rules, not StarNet defaults, Etsy ranking guarantees or universal manufacturing specifications. The selected print provider's actual product requirements control production dimensions and limits.

1 Design intent before generation

Every item needs a short written brief before artwork is generated. Save:

Buyer: a specific audience, such as readers who love cats.

Buying reason: self-expression, a gift, a book-club event or a seasonal occasion.

Product: exact garment or other blank, provider, colors and relevant variants.

Main idea: one sentence describing what the buyer should recognize.

Exact wording: approved text, capitalization and punctuation, or an explicit no-text decision.

Style: named visual direction and approved references.

Composition: illustrated graphic, typography, small emblem or another deliberate layout.

Print requirements: actual printable area and intended physical artwork size.

Things to avoid: relevant rejected examples and their reasons.

Do not begin with a vague request such as make something cozy and let every agent invent a different interpretation. Missing technical data must be resolved or marked blocked. Missing nonessential creative details can use the saved collection defaults.

3 Composition and hierarchy

The viewer should identify the main idea before noticing decoration. Establish a clear order: main illustration or headline, supporting element, optional minor text.

Give the main subject enough space to remain recognizable when reduced.

Keep visible breathing room between text, ears, leaves, books and borders.

Avoid accidental tangencies: objects barely touching can create confusing shapes.

Do not squeeze a long phrase into the available width by distorting letters.

Keep optical balance. A design can be mathematically centered but look off-center because the illustration's mass sits to one side.

Use symmetry only when it serves the concept. Do not mirror objects so mechanically that the picture looks duplicated or anatomically strange.

Avoid oversized empty margins inside the art file. They can make an apparently large upload print as a tiny graphic.

Do not solve every weakness by adding more detail or distressing.

A small emblem is allowed when the brief deliberately calls for one. A larger graphic is allowed when it fits the product and audience. Neither small nor large is an automatic quality verdict.

4 Typography and wording

Treat every letter as content that must be correct, not decorative texture.

Use exact approved wording. Check spelling, apostrophes, punctuation, spaces, repeated words and missing letters.

Prefer one font family, with an optional contrasting second family. This is a collection default, not a manufacturing limit.

Keep a short headline distinct from any secondary line. Do not make three text blocks compete equally.

Avoid very thin outlines, cramped counters, compressed letterforms and decorative scripts that obscure the words.

Check curved lettering letter by letter. Avoid uneven baselines, distorted end letters and collisions with the illustration.

Remove a secondary line if it adds little or becomes unreadable at the intended physical size.

Do not require every tiny secondary word to read in a thumbnail. It must still print clearly at actual size, and the product's main message must work without enlarging the listing.

For important copy, prefer generating the illustration separately and setting text with a reliable text/vector tool when the workflow supports it. Inspect the final composed file afterward.

OCR can flag discrepancies, but it cannot replace visual inspection of stylized lettering. Uncertain letters require correction or human review.

Use original, audience-appropriate wording. Do not mistake familiar phrases for proof of demand or originality.

No mockup or attractive illustration can compensate for a misspelled headline.

5 Illustration and AI defect checks

Inspect the actual saved artwork, including at full resolution. For a cat-and-books design, explicitly check:

Eyes and pupils are coherent, with intentionally chosen symmetry or asymmetry.

Ears, muzzle and whiskers have believable placement.

Paws do not fuse into books, disappear ambiguously or acquire unexplained extra digits.

The tail connects plausibly and does not become a book spine, branch or second limb.

The open book has coherent covers, page edges and a central fold.

Stacked books have consistent outlines rather than warped blocks or accidental text.

Foliage connects to stems; leaves do not emerge from the animal's face or dissolve into lettering.

Tiny generated marks do not resemble unwanted writing, signatures or watermarks.

Shadows do not turn intended forms into holes or blobs.

No stray object is present merely because the image model generated it.

Stylization can simplify anatomy. It does not excuse an accidental, confusing result. If the reviewer cannot explain an unusual shape as a deliberate part of the design, revise it or flag it.

6 Detail, line weight and texture

Judge details at intended print size, not only while zoomed in on a large monitor.

Maintain clear separation between adjacent shapes and between dark areas.

Enlarge or simplify important fine elements when they disappear at physical size.

Avoid dense stacks of thin lines in fur, leaves and book pages.

Keep distressing restrained and deliberate. It must not erase letters, eyes, outlines or key story elements.

Do not use blur or fake paper texture to disguise generation artifacts.

Match the production method. An illustration acceptable for a garment print may be unsuitable for embroidery or engraving.

There is no universal minimum line thickness for every provider and print method. Obtain the relevant specification or validate with a sample before treating fine detail as proven.

7 Placement and size on the actual product

Check the complete mockup, not only the artwork canvas. Artwork that looks centered on a square can sit too low on a sweatshirt.

Choose the intended placement explicitly: centered chest, small emblem, back print or another supported position.

Use the real print area and product dimensions. Do not derive inches from a photographed screen.

For a standard centered chest design, aim for an upper-chest composition that looks intentional relative to the neckline and garment body.

Keep the illustration away from seams, ribbing, pockets and unsupported print regions.

Check visible artwork bounds as well as file bounds. Transparent margins must not distort positioning.

Preserve aspect ratio. Never stretch the subject to fill the print area.

Record artwork width and height in physical units, print position, product/provider and approved version. Reuse approved placement for matching products.

Check relevant size variants. Do not assume the visible print scales identically for every garment size or provider.

Reassess placement when changing a sweatshirt to a tote, mug or another product; copying coordinates is not adaptation.

If uncertain, compare two controlled mockups that differ in one variable. For example, keep placement fixed while testing scale, then keep scale fixed while testing height. The best choice comes from the product preview and intended style, not a blanket bigger-is-better rule.

8 Specific lesson from the first sweatshirt mockup

The photographed mockup showed a warm neutral sweatshirt with a cat-and-book graphic, an arched headline and a small bottom line. It is a useful starting direction, not an approved master.

Observed concerns: the artwork appeared low and relatively small, while the secondary line was difficult to read in the photo. The source image is needed to establish sharpness and fine illustration quality.

Next revision: compare a higher placement, compare the existing graphic with a moderately larger version, and test enlarged or removed secondary text. Inspect the headline's outlined lettering against a stronger alternative. Keep the audience and overall warm palette consistent during those tests.

The earlier suggestion to try about 25% larger is one comparison for this mockup, not a permanent rule for every future design. Do not infer a universal chest offset, print width or quality score from this photograph.

9 Contrast and garment colors

Review the real artwork on every garment color offered for sale.

Dark artwork must remain separated from a dark garment. Cream detail must not disappear on cream fabric.

Do not add a white outline automatically. Use one only when it belongs to the visual style and solves the contrast issue cleanly.

Verify that a removed background has not also removed light details inside the artwork.

Do not rely on subtle screen-only color differences to define important shapes.

Restrict variants to colors that work. More color options are not a reason to approve unreadable combinations.

Save a separate approved artwork version when adapting ink colors; regenerate the correct mockup and review it again.

10 Transparency, cropping and file integrity

For isolated apparel graphics, use a genuinely transparent background when the chosen method requires it.

Inspect the alpha channel rather than trusting the filename or generator's claim.

Composite the file over white, dark and strongly colored backgrounds for internal checks. Look for opaque rectangles, checkerboard patterns, halos, fragments and missing interior details.

Preserve intentional semi-transparency; distinguish it from accidental faded edges.

Do not cut off whiskers, leaves, letter edges, shadows or decorative marks during cropping.

Trim excessive transparent padding while preserving required breathing room and alignment.

Confirm that the final uploaded file is the inspected version. A preview of one version does not clear another file.

Do not submit a screenshot, photographed monitor, product mockup or JPEG of the whole sweatshirt as the print artwork.

11 Print readiness

Obtain the exact product/provider and print-area requirements from Printify's Product Creator or the supplier's current specification.

Verify supported format, pixel dimensions, intended physical size and file size.

For raster art, assess effective resolution at print size. Example: 3000 useful artwork pixels across a 10-inch-wide print gives 300 pixels per inch. This arithmetic is an example, not a universal print requirement.

Changing a DPI metadata field does not create detail. Upscaled pixels can still contain soft edges and distorted letters.

Inspect source sharpness and final output separately. Do not approve because the file has large dimensions alone.

Preserve color handling appropriate to the supplier's specification; do not assume a conversion is required merely because another printer uses it.

Verify actual SVG contents where vector output is used. An SVG wrapper containing a blurry bitmap does not make it resolution-independent.

Treat a new product, provider or print technique as needing fresh production validation. A sample is the strongest check of physical output; obtain spending authorization before ordering one.

Printify recommends product-specific dimensions and commonly recommends 300 DPI for raster work, with exceptions for some products. Its available print area is a maximum printable region, not an instruction to fill every inch. See the sources at the end.

12 Product mockups and listing images

The mockup is evidence of the proposed product, so it must use the correct artwork, garment, color, print location and approximate physical scale.

Prefer provider mockups or appropriately licensed product photography that accurately represents the offered product.

Do not let a generated lifestyle scene redraw the design, improve the garment, change its cut or invent printing effects.

Do not show embroidery, raised ink, foil, texture or stitching when the ordered method will not produce that effect.

Include a clear overall product view and a useful closer view of the design where appropriate.

Avoid crops that obscure the product's identity or conceal important placement details.

Keep the product visually dominant. Props and scenery must not hide the artwork.

Use clean exports without browser chrome, cursors, editor controls, monitor reflections or measurement guides.

Keep listing variants consistent with their photos. Do not use a cream preview for an otherwise unpictured dark variant that has not been checked.

Follow Etsy's current image requirements for the product category. Authorized stock mockups are permitted for qualifying print-on-demand items; that does not make every AI-generated product image acceptable.

13 Thumbnail and viewing tests

Use three separate views. Each answers a different question:

View

Question

Full-resolution artwork

Are letters, edges, shapes and transparency correct?

Actual product mockup

Are placement, scale and garment contrast right?

Small listing preview

Can a shopper recognize the product and main idea quickly?

For a repeatable internal thumbnail test, reduce the final primary listing image to about 200 pixels wide. This is our test size, not an Etsy-mandated dimension.

The product and main subject should remain recognizable. If the headline is essential to the concept, it should remain legible enough to communicate. If the design only works when enlarged, simplify the illustration or improve the crop and scale. Do not make the mockup misleadingly large just to pass the test.

Also inspect the garment preview at normal viewing size. Neither a beautiful close-up nor a readable thumbnail proves print quality.

14 Originality and collection consistency

Use research to understand audiences, occasions and visual preferences. Create a new composition and wording rather than tracing a competing listing.

Use assets and fonts with appropriate commercial rights; retain their provenance.

Escalate uncertain rights rather than asserting that AI generation makes them safe.

Keep the collection's palette, illustration treatment, typography and subject language consistent.

Vary the actual idea. Changing a cat's color or swapping one word does not automatically justify a new listing.

Compare against the owner's approved collection examples and the shop's existing designs. Do not use a competitor's bestseller as the master to reproduce.

Do not promise that a design will sell because it passed visual review. Visual quality, demand evidence and profitability are separate judgments.

16 Review sequence and verdicts

Read the brief, selected product requirements, approved reference and relevant saved feedback.

Open the actual source artwork and inspect full-resolution details.

Check text, subject integrity, composition, contrast and originality/provenance concerns.

Verify transparency, visible bounds, file properties and effective print size.

Inspect the real product mockups and relevant variants.

Run the thumbnail test and compare with the approved style direction.

Record defects with locations, evidence and specific corrections.

Recheck every category after revision and identify the exact version reviewed.

Use these verdicts:

PASS TO OWNER: all mandatory checks passed; the complete product is ready for the owner's creative decision. This does not authorize publication.

REVISE: identified defects have a clear correction the designer can make. Return specific instructions automatically.

BLOCKED: a missing source file, missing manufacturing specification, uncertain material issue or exhausted revision limit prevents a reliable decision. Tell the owner exactly what is needed.

REJECT CONCEPT: the concept does not meet the brief or collection's requirements and needs a new direction rather than cosmetic repair.

Suggested initial revision policy: allow up to three attempts to fix a specific failed design, then escalate with the best candidate and unresolved issues. This is our proposed operating limit, not a StarNet rule. Do not mark the item approved merely because the attempts ran out.

Do not average away a critical failure with an overall score. A beautiful composition with the wrong wording, a defective file or a misleading mockup still fails the relevant gate.

Use descriptions such as the bottom line is unreadable at the selected print size; increase it or remove it. Avoid vague instructions such as make it premium, fix the AI look or try harder.

If a check is subjective, say it is a visual judgment. If it requires a tool or a physical sample, state the evidence used. Never claim physical production was verified from a digital preview.

18 Feedback that prevents repeated mistakes

Store the owner's decision, the affected version, what they liked, the defect, the correction and the scope of the lesson. Distinguish collection-wide preferences from a one-product exception.

Examples of useful saved lessons:

For centered sweatshirt graphics in this collection, compare placement against approved garment reference A before showing the owner.

Remove nonessential microtext when it cannot print clearly at the chosen physical size.

Keep the warm charcoal, cream and terracotta palette unless the brief calls for a different collection.

A black cat on dark fabric requires a specifically checked adaptation; do not enable every color by default.

Do not convert a single preference into an unrelated permanent ban. Approval of a small emblem does not mean all designs should be small. Rejection of one cat drawing does not mean cats are prohibited.

Keep approved masters unchanged. Save revised candidates as new versions. A material change to artwork, scale, placement, garment color or mockup needs the applicable checks and owner approval again. Historical style approval does not automatically approve a new product.

The designer must read relevant saved lessons before the next generation, and the reviewer must explicitly check the recurring defect. Otherwise the feedback exists only as a log and has not improved the process.

19 Final acceptance checklist

The intended buyer and main idea are clear.

The artwork is original enough to satisfy the brief and has appropriate asset provenance.

Exact wording is correct and important text is readable.

No unexplained anatomy, geometry or generation defects remain.

Composition, contrast and detail work at the intended size.

Placement is checked on the actual product, not inferred from the square artwork.

Offered garment variants are checked.

Print file properties meet the selected provider's actual requirements.

Transparency and cropping are verified where applicable.

Mockups match the exact artwork and product version.

Thumbnail and full-resolution checks both passed their separate purposes.

Unverified production outcomes are identified honestly.

The owner reviewed the specific product and version.

Publishing authorization is handled separately by the workflow.

Any unmet mandatory item means the product is not ready to publish. Passing this checklist establishes internal readiness for the next authorized step; it does not certify market demand, profit or Etsy policy compliance by itself.
